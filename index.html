02 — Audit Evidence Gap

A framework document is not runtime evidence

Advisory frameworks tell your regulator what you planned to govern. Kinetic's Cryptographic Execution Ledger tells your regulator what your agents actually did — in SHA-256 hash-chained, mathematically verifiable form. Any auditor can verify the record without Kinetic's involvement.

03 — Execution Boundary

In-process governance can be overwritten

Self-evolving AI agents can modify in-process governance controls from within their own execution environment. Kinetic operates at the network layer — structurally outside the agent's reach. The isolation is architectural, not configurable.

04 — Design-Time vs. Runtime

Assessment compliance ≠ execution compliance

Governance certifications confirm that an assessment methodology was followed at design time. Kinetic confirms that governance controls executed correctly at runtime, on every agent call, in production. Regulators require both. Most enterprises currently have only one.

⚖ OSFI Guideline E-23 — Final Version, September 2025

OSFI's final Guideline E-23, effective May 1, 2027, applies to all federally regulated financial institutions and all AI models — regardless of whether they are built internally or sourced from a third party. It requires an enterprise-wide, risk-based framework governing every stage of the model lifecycle, including ongoing monitoring and review throughout production. A point-in-time compliance assessment does not satisfy this requirement. Kinetic is the runtime monitoring layer that makes your AI governance posture continuously defensible at examination — not just at the moment of assessment.
